# Slide 02 — The Problem

## THE PROBLEM

### Great campaigns are still assembled manually.

A small brand team may have the idea, the product, and the audience, but turning that into a coordinated campaign means jumping between strategy, copywriting, design, video, scheduling, analytics, and approvals.

### The real workflow today

```text
Brief
 ↓
Strategy document
 ↓
Copywriting tool
 ↓
Design tool
 ↓
Video / audio tool
 ↓
Spreadsheet / calendar
 ↓
Social platforms
 ↓
Analytics dashboard
```

Every handoff loses context.

### What it costs

- Hours spent converting one idea into multiple platform formats
- Brand context gets lost between tools
- Content becomes repetitive or generic
- Campaign planning is disconnected from content creation
- AI-generated content can make unsupported claims
- Teams have little visibility into why an asset was created
- Performance data rarely feeds directly back into the next creative decision

### Visual

Show a fragmented toolchain on the left collapsing into one Campaign Launchpad workspace on the right.

# Slide 03 — Solution Overview

## THE SOLUTION

### Campaign Launchpad is the campaign command center.

The user gives it:

**One paragraph + brand context + campaign goal**

The system coordinates:

**Strategy → Creative direction → Content → Assets → Approval → Launch → Measurement**

### What the user does

1. Create a campaign
2. Add a short campaign brief
3. Attach brand/product context
4. Collaborate with the AI strategist
5. Select a creative direction
6. Generate and edit platform-specific content
7. Review the campaign timeline
8. Approve assets
9. Run experiments
10. Monitor performance and capture learnings

### What they get

- Campaign strategy
- Audience + positioning
- Platform-specific content
- Images and creative assets
- Voice / audio content
- Social media launch timeline
- Human approval workflow
- A/B experiments
- Campaign analytics
- Traceable generation history
- Campaign learnings for future launches

### Core product promise

> **The AI doesn't just create content. It keeps the campaign coherent from the first idea to the final result.**

- Understands the campaign goal
- Defines positioning and audience
- Suggests creative directions

- Creates variants and assets

- Builds the launch timeline
- Maps content to campaign stages
- Suggests experiments and distribution

**Evaluator / Quality Gate**
- Checks grounding
- Checks brand consistency
- Checks platform fit
- Catches unsupported claims
- Routes failures back for revision

# Slide 06 — Key Features

## ONE WORKSPACE. THE WHOLE CAMPAIGN.

### 01 — AI Campaign Strategist
Conversationally turn a raw idea into positioning, audience, messaging, creative directions, and campaign goals.

### 02 — Brand Brain
Upload brand documents, PDFs, previous campaigns, product information, and voice references. Retrieve grounded context whenever content is generated.

### 03 — Campaign Canvas
A visual workspace for creating, editing, regenerating, and organizing content across Instagram, LinkedIn, X, video, audio, and visual assets.

### 04 — Marketing Timeline
Generate a campaign-specific launch plan with content mapped to campaign stages, platforms, dates, and objectives.

### 05 — Creative Studio
Generate platform-native copy, images, audio / voice content, video concepts, hooks, captions, thumbnails, and variants.

### 06 — Human Approval
Review, approve, reject, or request targeted changes at every important stage.

### 07 — A/B Experiments
Create content variants, track their performance, and feed observed campaign learnings back into future creative decisions.

### 08 — Quality Gate + Trace
Show what context was retrieved, what the system generated, what failed evaluation, and what changed before approval.

### 09 — Campaign Memory
After launch, preserve useful learnings such as successful creative patterns, audience responses, and underperforming formats.

# Slide 08 — Baseline & Evaluation

## HOW WE PROVE IT WORKS

The hackathon specifically asks for a baseline, a self-defined evaluation set, human approval, and traces / failures.

### Baseline

Compare:

**Generic AI workflow**
vs.
**Campaign Launchpad**

Possible measurements:

- Time from brief → first publishable campaign
- Number of manual steps
- Brand-context adherence
- Platform-format adherence
- Grounded claim rate
- Human approval pass rate
- Regeneration rate

### Evaluation set

Build a controlled test set of campaign briefs containing:

- Different brand voices
- Different campaign objectives
- Different platforms
- Different audiences
- Known product facts
- Deliberately tricky / conflicting context
- Examples containing unsupported claims

### Evaluation dimensions

```text
Brand consistency
Platform fit
Audience alignment
Source grounding
Factual claim accuracy
Creative diversity
CTA relevance
Human approval rate
```

### Failure testing

Intentionally test:

- Missing context
- Conflicting documents
- Unsupported claims
- Ambiguous campaign briefs
- Wrong platform requirements
- Tone mismatch

Then show the trace of detection → rejection → revision → approval.

### Important presentation principle

Do not invent final performance numbers before running the evaluation.

Use real measurements from the finished prototype.

# PART IV — DESIGN PRINCIPLES FOR THE DEMO

## 1. One visual language across every page

The dashboard, canvas, analytics, and trace viewer should look like one product.

Do not make each screen look like a different SaaS template.

---

## 2. Use the campaign as the primary object

Navigation should always make it obvious:

**Which campaign am I working on?**

Example top bar:

```text
CAMPAIGN LAUNCHPAD
/
SUMMER PRODUCT LAUNCH
/
CONTENT
```

---

## 3. Make AI actions visible

Instead of a magical spinner saying:

> "AI is thinking..."

show meaningful states:

```text
Retrieving brand context
Analyzing audience
Building campaign strategy
Generating creative direction
Checking claims
Preparing variants
```

This makes the agentic workflow legible during the demo.

---

## 4. Make provenance visible without overwhelming the user

Use small source indicators:

`Brand Guide §2`

`Product Brief §4`

`Audience Research §1`

Clicking one opens the evidence.

---

## 5. Avoid "AI score: 93"

Prefer explainable checks:

```text
Brand fit       ✓
Grounding       ✓
Platform fit    ✓
Audience fit    ✓
Claim check     ✓
```

If you use numerical evaluation metrics, reserve them for the Evaluation page where the methodology is explained.

---

## 6. Give every AI output a human escape hatch

Every major generated result should have:

**Edit · Regenerate · Explain · View sources · Approve**

The user should never feel trapped by the AI.
